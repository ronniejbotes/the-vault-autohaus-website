At The Vault in Bedfordview, frontal PPF starts at R15 000 and full PPF at R35 000. Matte film costs the same as clear, and colour PPF, a full colour change, starts at R40 000. Every PPF job comes with a complimentary detail, and no VAT is added. We can only speak for our own prices, so that's what this page covers: what you get for each price, what moves it, and how PPF compares with a ceramic coating.

What moves the price

How much of the car you cover

Frontal PPF covers the front of the car, where stone chips hit hardest: the complete front bumper, the entire bonnet (not just the leading edge), both front fenders and both painted mirror caps. Full PPF covers every painted panel, including the lower doors and sills that catch the grit your tyres throw up. That's the gap between R15 000 and R35 000.

The size of the car

A large SUV can cost more: up to R3 000 extra for clear PPF, and up to R5 000 extra for colour PPF.

Clear, matte or colour film

Matte film costs the same as clear. Clear lets your paint's colour and gloss show through. Matte turns gloss paint soft and flat without a respray, and the gloss is still there if the film comes off later. On a gloss car, though, the matte look only works if every painted panel is covered.

Colour PPF changes the colour of the whole car and protects the paint in the same job. It starts at R40 000 with the door shuts included. If your colour has to be ordered in, allow 2 to 3 weeks for it to arrive. You can try all 394 films in the colour changer first.

The state of your paint

Clear film shows what's underneath. Chips that are already there stay visible, and swirl marks are best polished out before the film goes on. If your car needs a polish first, polishing starts at R1 200. When we inspect the car, we'll tell you whether it needs one.

Frontal or full?

Frontal suits a daily driver that does a lot of highway or gravel kilometres. It protects the parts that chip first for less than half the price of full PPF. If you drive the N3, N12 or R24 around Bedfordview every day, these are the panels that take the stones.

Full PPF suits a new car, a car you plan to keep for years, or one where the original paint matters when you sell. If the budget is tight, frontal PPF covers the areas that chip most.

More on how the film works, and how we fit it, on our paint protection film page.

PPF or ceramic coating?

They do different jobs. PPF is a tough film that takes the stone chips instead of your paint. A ceramic coating is a thin, hard layer that adds gloss and makes the car quicker to wash. It won't stop a stone.

You don't have to choose one. A common setup is PPF on the front, where the chips happen, and a ceramic coating on the rest. We can also coat over gloss PPF, which makes it easier to clean. Over matte PPF, a coating would take away the matte effect. Ask us to price the combination.

How to compare PPF quotes

Two quotes only compare if they cover the same work. With them side by side, ask each shop:

  • Does "frontal" mean the entire bonnet, or only the front edge of it? Ours is the entire bonnet.
  • Are both front fenders and the mirror caps included? Ours covers both front fenders and both painted side-mirror caps.
  • Is VAT included, or added on top? We don't add VAT.
  • Is there a warranty, and for how long? Ours is 5 years.
  • Has anyone looked at the car? We inspect it before booking and then confirm the price.

It is if stone chips bother you, you drive long distances, or you want the original paint intact when you sell. If the budget is tight, frontal PPF covers the areas that chip most.
